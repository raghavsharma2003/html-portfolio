# Voice frontier and bounded Azure benchmark

Research date: 2026-09-27. Public primary-source review only. No model inference, GPU activation, cloud mutation, credential access, purchase or owner-audio upload occurred. Twelve principal external sources are linked below. Published capabilities and vendor measurements are not independently reproduced results.

## Decision

Do not replace the voice stack on a leaderboard claim. First compare the exact current baseline against current Chatterbox V3/Hindi and IndicF5 on the owner's voice. Preserve a narrow adapter boundary so a licensed frontier model can enter later without rewriting the person, memory or conversation layers.

The most relevant new findings are Chatterbox's dedicated Hindi pack, Cartesia Sonic 3.6's explicit Hinglish work, and Fish's distinction between hosted S2.1 Pro and released S2 Pro. Qwen3-TTS is real, but its official language coverage excludes Hindi. None establishes that Vyakti sounds like its owner, or beats Fish or ElevenLabs at that task.

## Three arms, then stop and decide

1. **A: exact existing baseline.** Freeze deployed image, model hashes, reference hash, transcript, language evidence and effective parameters. Preserve the historical anchor only for its exact reference. New people need their own calibration. This is the control, not an endorsement of its quality.
2. **B: current Chatterbox.** Use a tiny development set to choose V3 multilingual or the Hindi pack before the blinded test. Freeze that choice. Score English and within-sentence switching explicitly so a Hindi pronunciation win cannot hide identity drift or broken English. Do not stitch voices from different engines inside a sentence.
3. **C: IndicF5.** Use the same original owner reference and independently checked transcript. On development items only, compare original script with a reversible pronunciation representation; freeze normalization before evaluation and retain original text. Admit it as a Hindi/code-mix challenger, not a claimed universal replacement.

Frontier substitution is conditional, not a fourth immediate workstream. Prefer a quote/entitlement check for Sonic 3.6 Azure private serving because it explicitly targets Hinglish. Fish S2 Pro requires commercial rights; Fish S2.1 and ElevenLabs need verified Azure availability for this constraint. No emails, quote requests or procurement were sent. Qwen is an English fallback experiment only if English fidelity remains poor after the three-arm result.

**Reversal condition:** change this shortlist when a pinned, usable Azure artifact or licensed private endpoint demonstrates a materially better owner-blind result within the same budget. A vendor ranking or an attractive generic voice does not reverse it.

## Benchmark specification, not completed measurements

**Reference admission.** Retain native capture bandwidth and original bytes. Check actual spectrum, clipping, multiple speakers, noise and reference/transcript alignment; do not repeat the 16 kHz scoring-byte or unnecessary-separation mistakes. A spectrum score is an input diagnostic, never a likeness score. Prefer several clean, naturally spoken clips across the owner's Hindi and English styles. Hold out the owner's readings of test prompts from enrollment.

**Stage 1: bounded screening.** One consenting owner, 12 development prompts and 24 unseen test prompts: six Hindi, six Indian English, six Devanagari/English mixed-script, six Romanized Hinglish. Balance short replies and longer explanations; include names, numbers, technical vocabulary, questions, emphasis and mid-sentence switching. Three arms times 24 prompts times two fixed seeds gives 144 generated test clips. Development generations, failures and retries count separately against a predeclared compute cap. Do not cherry-pick a best seed. If the cap cannot cover 144 clips, reduce the declared design before starting and report its smaller n.

Use identical source material, equal tuning opportunity and loudness-matched playback without pitch or timbre processing. Give each engine its documented reference format while recording every transform. Randomize anonymous labels and order; preserve the model key outside the rater view. Compare against held-out natural owner speech and include an unrelated-voice control. Keep required disclosure and watermark in every generated clip; score a marked speech span consistently so disclosure is not a cue identifying one model.

**Human outcomes.** Owner plus at least three Hindi/English listeners familiar with the owner independently rate likeness, naturalness, accent, prosody and code-switch intelligibility on separate five-point scales. Also ask forced preference and whether the voice would be recognizable without the script. Obtain human transcripts for word/character error and inspect every switch boundary, number and name. Report owner opinion separately from other raters. Prosody includes pacing, pauses, emphasis and emotional fit, not just pitch similarity. Speaker embeddings are secondary diagnostics, calibrated against owner-vs-owner and unrelated speakers, never presented as a percentage clone fidelity.

**Latency and reliability.** Record request acceptance, ready text, synthesis start, first returned bytes, first playable non-silent audio, last audio, cancellation and browser playout. Report cold and warm separately, with GPU SKU, region, codec, text length and concurrency. Warm characterization needs at least 30 requests per selected arm at concurrency one and the proposed pilot concurrency; three cold starts are only descriptive. TTFA alone is not a conversation result. Measure user-end-of-turn to audible reply, interruption-to-silence and failed/stalled turns through the actual browser.

**Proposed advance criteria.** Likeness median at least 4/5, owner acceptance, no supported-language median below 3.5, no material number/name omission, and no significant intelligibility regression versus A. A challenge winner needs a paired improvement with uncertainty reported; one owner remains a personalized result. For a later broader claim, repeat held-out evaluation with at least eight consenting speakers and cluster uncertainty by speaker, prompt and rater. Repeated ratings are not independent people.

**Proposed real-time targets.** Warm p95 end-of-turn to audible reply at most 1.2 seconds; p95 interruption-to-silence at most 250 ms; sustained RTF below one at the intended concurrency. These are design targets, not existing measurements. Reject a real-time arm if it cannot meet them within the agreed cost ceiling; retain it for asynchronous clips only if the owner values its quality. Stop after one bounded reference repair and one bounded parameter pass if likeness remains below the floor. Investigate data quality before financing fine-tuning.

## Full duplex and cost

Streaming TTS does not supply listening, turn-taking, memory, personality or full-duplex behavior. Vyakti still needs incremental ASR, echo-aware turn detection, interruption/cancellation that stops queued playback, response generation tied to the person, and measured recovery when the user resumes speaking. Treat TTS, conversation semantics and duplex orchestration as separate measured components. Model-generated dialogue between multiple voices does not prove a model can listen while speaking.

Use a streaming ASR -> person/memory-aware response -> incremental TTS path as the first testable architecture. Preserve the owner's text as the semantic record; pronunciation transforms belong at the speech boundary. Measure how text chunk size trades first-audio time against natural phrasing and voice continuity. Native speech-to-speech can be revisited when an Azure-deployable candidate proves owner-conditioned Hindi/Hinglish, interruptions and accountable memory better under the same test.

For each Azure run record allocated GPU seconds including warm-up and idle tails, effective SKU rate, CPU/RAM, model download/build time, storage, egress and any license fee. Compute **gross cost per accepted output minute = total attributed run cost / accepted audible minutes**. Also report total generated minutes, rejection/retry rate, concurrent active sessions and cost per conversation minute including ASR and response model. Low traffic can make idle allocation dominate. Credits reduce cash payable, not resource cost; report gross consumption and credited consumption separately. Subscription credit balance, eligibility, quota and enterprise-license coverage remain unknown until verified for the actual offer. Never extrapolate an H200 demo into this subscription's costs.
