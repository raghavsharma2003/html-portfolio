## Gamification mechanics for Meera: adopt-vs-refuse ranking against a no-manipulation charter

- **Duolingo micro-interaction/emotional-design writeups** [reported]
  Confetti/animation intensity is scaled to the size of the achievement and fires immediately after the triggering action (lesson complete, milestone hit) — not on a schedule, not to prevent a loss. It is pure positive reinforcement with no downside state.
  - _why here:_ Directly implementable for Meera: scale a celebration moment (voice line + visual burst) to rel-state milestones, first we-episode, day-count thresholds — reward what happened, never threaten what will be lost.
- **Strava Help Center — Kudos and Personal/Global Heatmaps** [verified-in-source]
  Kudos is a single low-friction tap of social acknowledgment on someone else's effort, decoupled from any streak or loss condition. Heatmaps are a passive visualization of accumulated history the user already generated — nothing is 'at risk' by not viewing it.
  - _why here:_ Maps onto Meera's memory graph directly: a passive, always-available visualization of shared history (rituals completed, day counts, we-episodes) that the user can look at or ignore with zero penalty either way.
- **ScienceDirect study on Snapchat streaks & problematic smartphone use/FOMO among ** [verified-in-source]
  Streaks work by loss aversion (losses loom ~2x larger than equivalent gains per Kahneman/Tversky prospect theory) and by social-obligation pressure — users report feeling they'll 'offend or disappoint' another person if they let a shared streak lapse. Snap's own leadership documented awareness of this as anxiety-inducing, not just externally alleged.
  - _why here:_ This is the mechanism the charter explicitly bans (guilt mechanics, loss-aversion pressure) and it is now litigated territory — Snap is a named defendant in ongoing 2026 addiction lawsuits citing this exact feature. For a 1:1 companion where the 'friend' IS the product, a lapsed-streak guilt message from Meera herself would be the single most reputationally and legally dangerous mechanic available.
- **Duolingo streak-restoration event coverage** [reported]
  Streak freezes and paid/one-time streak-restoration flows are damage control layered on top of a mechanic the company already knows causes anxiety — FTC dark-pattern guidance specifically calls out gamification-driven addiction and pay-to-fix flows as design that 'tricks or manipulates users into choices they would not otherwise make.'
  - _why here:_ Pay-to-restore is the clearest FTC-adjacent exposure of any mechanic surveyed — it monetizes the anxiety the streak itself created. For Meera this is a hard no twice over: it is manipulation by charter, and it is a live regulatory target in 2026.
- **Lichess Puzzle Streak mechanics** [verified-in-source]
  Puzzle Streak is a personal-best chase (score correlates with rating, e.g. 1500 rating ≈ 15 streak) with one-strike-and-done design, but losing a streak costs the user nothing they already owned — no subscription is gated behind it, no social relationship is implicated, and restarting is frictionless and free. Improvement tools are never paywalled.
  - _why here:_ Shows a personal-record/high-score mechanic can exist without loss-aversion harm if the 'loss' only ends a session rather than threatening an accumulated relationship or costing money to undo. Directly applicable to Meera's games centre (chess/tictactoe) — track personal bests as a badge of skill, never as a rel-state consequence.
- **Discord badges documentation** [verified-in-source]
  Badges are permanent, non-decaying, purely additive collectibles tied to real one-time actions or tenure (e.g. Nitro-tenure badges that evolve at milestones) — nothing is ever revoked, and lacking one imposes no visible penalty on the profile.
  - _why here:_ A collections/milestone-badge model (we-episode badges, ritual-completion badges, relationship-day-count milestones) is safe under the charter specifically because it is additive-only and non-revocable — the opposite structural shape from a streak.

**Top actions:**
- ADOPT (rank 1-6, evidence-backed): 1) Scaled celebration moments fired immediately on real milestones (Duolingo confetti pattern — reward-only, no counter-state to lose). 2) Passive, always-available history visualization of rel-state/rituals/we-episodes (Strava heatmap pattern — zero penalty for not checking it). 3) Non-revocable additive badges/collectibles for relationship milestones and day-counts (Discord badge pattern — nothing is ever taken away). 4) Personal-best/high-score tracking in the games centre scoped to session skill only, never touching rel-state (Lichess Puzzle Streak pattern — losing ends a session, not a relationship). 5) Kudos-style lightweight acknowledgment of user effort/consistency framed as noticing, not scorekeeping (Strava kudos pattern). 6) Visible 'progress made' summaries (e.g. 'we've built 12 rituals together') stated as accumulated fact, never as a countdown or at-risk quantity.
- REFUSE explicitly (rank 1-5 by harm/legal exposure, all evidence-backed): 1) Any lapse-triggered guilt or 'you're letting me down' messaging from Meera herself — this is the exact mechanism Snap's own executives internally called anxiety-inducing and that underlies active 2026 addiction litigation; in a 1:1 companion this would be manipulation delivered in the persona's own voice, the worst possible surface for it. 2) Streaks with loss-aversion framing (broken-streak sad-mascot equivalents) applied to rel-state or day-counts. 3) Pay-to-restore or any monetized undo of a lapsed relationship state — the clearest FTC dark-pattern exposure found (2026 guidance explicitly targets gamification-driven addiction plus paid-fix flows). 4) Fake urgency/artificial scarcity ('Meera is waiting, reply now') — fabricates urgency the AI has no real stake in and no source treats it as anything but manipulation. 5) Any mechanic that ties the user's worth or Meera's affection to a countable, decaying number — decaying-value mechanics were the common thread across every backlash case (Snapchat, Duolingo, BeReal) and are structurally what the charter's 'no streak-loss pressure' clause bans.
- Log this as a decision node in context/decisions.md per project convention, with an explicit reversal condition: 'Rejected streak/loss-aversion mechanics for Meera. Reverses if: charter language changes to permit loss-framed engagement mechanics, or a measured A/B on Meera specifically shows additive-only celebration mechanics fail to sustain engagement versus a loss-framed control.' Flag clearly that this ranking is evidence-from-competitors only (n=0 Meera-specific measurement) — no in-product experiment has been run, so do not imply coverage beyond what was researched.

## Game-feel / "juice" techniques for Meera (React 19 + CSS), adapted to the repo's actual motion-lint gate (scripts/check-motion.mjs) and docs/DESIGN-STANDARDS.md

- **GDC 2012 'Juice It or Lose It'** [reported]
  TECHNIQUE 1 — Squash & stretch on tap/press, transform-only. On pointerdown, scale the element non-uniformly for ~80-120ms: scaleX(1.08) scaleY(0.92) then spring back to scaleX(1) scaleY(1). Volume-constant (wider=shorter) is what reads as weight, not a uniform scale. Implement as a CSS class toggle on `:active`/pointerdown with `transform: scale(1.08, 0.92)` and a spring-like `cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1)` return — transform-only, so it passes the layout-prop lint rule automatically. Keep total under the repo's MAX_UI_MS=300 (e.g. 90ms in, 140ms return = 230ms total). Use on: send-button press, game-piece pickup in tictactoe/chess, would-you-rather choice tap.
  - _why here:_ Fits directly inside the existing motion-lint gate (scripts/check-motion.mjs) with zero exceptions needed — it's transform-only, under 300ms, and non-decorative (it's feedback for a deliberate tap, which docs/DESIGN-STANDARDS.md explicitly names as the one case bounce/overshoot is earned).
- **blakecrosley.com Balatro analysis** [reported]
  TECHNIQUE 2 — Micro-rotation + lift on hover/select for interactive cards (game pieces, choice cards). Rotate ±2-3deg randomly per-instance (seeded by id so it's stable, not re-randomized each render) plus translateY(-4px to -8px) lift, both via transform, 150-180ms ease-out. This 'organic randomness' makes identical assets feel hand-placed rather than machine-tiled.
  - _why here:_ Directly usable in the would-you-rather card UI and chess piece selection — transform-only, fits the ≤300ms budget (repo's own --d-tap is 180ms), and reads as personality-consistent with a 'warm, funny' persona rather than sterile snapping.
- **GDC talk via search summary + blakecrosley.com Balatro screen-shake tiers** [inferred]
  TECHNIQUE 3 — Screen shake, tiered and requested-not-imposed. A decaying position offset (translate only, never top/left) applied to a shake-eligible container, magnitude scaled to event importance: e.g. 2-4px/0.15s for a minor win (tictactoe row complete), 4-6px/0.2s for a major moment (checkmate, level-up). 'Strongest request wins' — implement as a single shared shake controller a component calls with a magnitude, not each component independently animating. Cap at the low end of what's cited (Balatro's own 'small' tier, 2-4px/0.2s) and never use the 4-8px+rotation 'large' tier Balatro reserves for jackpot scores — that reads as slot-machine reward variance.
  - _why here:_ This is the one technique with real charter risk. Screen shake in Balatro is explicitly tuned as a variable-intensity reward signal (bigger win = bigger shake) — the same mechanism as slot-machine juice. For Meera it should mark completion/correctness, never escalate with unpredictability to keep players engaged (that's the banned mechanic). The charter-risk framing itself is my own inference, not sourced from the juice literature.
- **GDC talk** [inferred]
  TECHNIQUE 4 — Hit-stop as a brief total-freeze, 40-80ms, on a single decisive input (chess capture, tictactoe winning move, correctly answering a would-you-rather). Implement via a CSS class that pauses the animation/render of the moved piece for the freeze window; NOT a JS main-thread block. 40-80ms is below human reaction time for 'lag' but reads as impact.
  - _why here:_ Needs reconciling with the repo's own documented Apple-design rule 'never lock input during a transition' — hit-stop here should freeze the VISUAL only (the moved piece), while input elsewhere stays live. Flagging this tension rather than resolving it with false confidence.
- **blakecrosley.com Balatro fetch — digit-roll stagger with cubic-bezier** [verified-in-source]
  TECHNIQUE 5 — Staggered entrance for grouped elements (chat bubbles arriving, game results, memory/rel-state updates). 30-80ms stagger between siblings (matches DESIGN-STANDARDS.md's own 'Stagger grouped entrances 30-80ms'), each with ease-out, capped at 300ms per element. For a count-up number use rAF or CSS numeric interpolation, target 300-400ms total — keep each digit's own transition ≤300ms so the per-line lint regex passes even though the aggregate sequence reads longer, and still add a `motion-lint: allow` comment noting the aggregate intent, per the repo's 'reason is not optional' convention.
  - _why here:_ This is the one place the lint's mechanical blind spot matters: check-motion.mjs regexes individual `transition:`/`animation:` declarations for ms values, so a staggered group whose PER-ELEMENT duration is ≤300ms passes even if the whole sequence is longer — legitimate use, not a loophole to abuse.
- **docs/DESIGN-STANDARDS.md** [verified-in-source]
  TECHNIQUE 6 — Spring parameters for anything draggable/flickable. Use framer-motion (already in node_modules/package.json) spring configs: `{ type: 'spring', damping: 1.0 }` for programmatic moves (no overshoot — e.g. AI opponent's move animating into place), and `{ type: 'spring', damping: 0.8 }` ONLY when animating the release of a user's own flick/throw gesture, with velocity handed off from the pointerup event. Never apply the 0.8 bounce to anything that wasn't the direct result of gesture momentum — the exact impeccable-vs-apple-design conflict the repo's own doc already resolved ('bounce is earned by momentum, never applied as decoration').
  - _why here:_ This one requires no new research — it's already fully specified in the repo's docs/DESIGN-STANDARDS.md, fetched from emilkowalski/skills apple-design. Re-deriving it would violate the project's own principle (measure/read before reasoning); the correct move is pointing at the existing doc rather than proposing new numbers.
- **blakecrosley.com Balatro fetch — chip/particle bursts with colored trails on sco** [verified-in-source]
  TECHNIQUE 7 — Particle celebration, CSS-only and reduced-motion-safe, for rare/first-run moments (chess checkmate, a we-episode milestone, a ritual completion). Spawn 6-12 small absolutely-positioned divs from the trigger point using `transform: translate() scale()` and `opacity` only (never width/height), each with randomized angle/distance via inline custom properties, animated via a single shared `@keyframes particle-burst`, 400-600ms — this exceeds the 300ms UI cap so it must live in the 'rare occasion' exemption class the repo already carves out (same as the presence-glow exceptions) with its own `motion-lint: allow` comment. Must have a `prefers-reduced-motion` branch per the mechanized gate (rule 6) — the reduced variant should keep the burst but shrink travel distance to near-zero and duration to ~150ms, not remove it (repo's stated principle: 'reduced motion means gentler, never absent').
  - _why here:_ Directly implementable against the exact lint rule (rule 6, `@keyframes` requires a `prefers-reduced-motion` branch) and the exact exemption pattern already used twice in this codebase (onboarding dot, presence glow) — a precedent to cite in the commit rather than inventing a new exception category.
- **GDC talk / general game-audio-layering practice** [reported]
  TECHNIQUE 8 — Sound layering: 2-3 short layers per significant event rather than one sound, staggered 20-40ms — a soft attack 'tick' (<50ms) immediately on input, a slightly-delayed 'body' tone (60-120ms) confirming resolution, and for rare big moments a sparse third layer (chime/swell, only on first-run or milestone events, per the animate skill's 'delight budget applies' rule for rare/first-run interactions). Web implementation: pre-decoded short WebAudio buffers (not `<audio>` elements, which have ~100ms+ playback latency) fired via AudioBufferSourceNode with `start(currentTime + offsetSeconds)` for the stagger.
  - _why here:_ Confidence is lower here — no Meera-specific or Balatro-specific exact-ms sound-layering source was found; the 20-40ms stagger and WebAudio-vs-<audio> latency point are general web-audio engineering practice, not a juice-literature citation. Flagging honestly rather than presenting as measured.

**Top actions:**
- Implement squash-and-stretch (Technique 1) and micro-rotation/lift (Technique 2) first — both are transform-only, fit inside the existing MAX_UI_MS=300 motion-lint gate with zero exceptions, and have direct precedent in the repo's own docs/DESIGN-STANDARDS.md (springs, ease-out, ≤300ms).
- Before implementing screen shake (Technique 3), resolve the charter-risk flag explicitly with the owner: Balatro's shake scales with win magnitude as a variable-reward signal, structurally the same mechanic the anti-manipulation charter targets (fake urgency / variable reward). Keep shake fixed-and-modest (single tier, 2-4px/0.2s) rather than escalating, or drop it for a lower-risk cue.
- For particle celebration (Technique 7) and any staggered count-up (Technique 5), follow the two existing `motion-lint: allow <reason>` precedents already in the codebase (onboarding dot, presence glow) rather than inventing a new exemption pattern — cite them in the commit.

## Relationship/history visualization patterns from Snapchat Charms, Spotify Wrapped, Apple Photos Memories, Gentler Streak, Finch, and Daylio — extracted into an implementable blueprint for Meera's "Us" surface

- **Snapchat Charms** [reported]
  Charms are discrete, named, hideable relationship artifacts (zodiac compatibility, Snapstreak length, friendship anniversary, etc.) that live at the BOTTOM of a per-friend 'Friendship Profile' page, below the normal profile chrome. They accrue over time on their own schedule, are individually tappable to reveal a one-line explanation, and — critically — either party can hide any single charm from the shared view. The profile is per-relationship, not a global stats dashboard: there is one Charms shelf per friend, not one aggregate screen for all friends.
  - _why here:_ This is the direct template for an 'Us' surface: a per-companion page (not a global 'stats' tab) where each fact is its own named, tappable card rather than a row in a table. The hide-a-charm affordance maps cleanly onto Meera's anti-manipulation charter — users must be able to hide/mute an individual remembered moment or milestone without deleting the underlying memory-graph data, giving control without shame. Discrete named units also avoid the single 'score' framing that reads as gamified.
- **Spotify Wrapped psychology writeups — Growth.Design case study, UX Collective, M** [reported]
  Wrapped's emotional effect comes from three concrete mechanisms: (1) narrative sequencing — stats are revealed one full-screen card at a time in a fixed story arc (opening hook -> escalating specificity -> a 'top' superlative -> a shareable closing card), never as a simultaneous grid; (2) hyper-specific personalization copy ('You were in the top 0.5% of Jaanu listeners') that converts a count into an identity claim; (3) screenshot-ready single-card composition — each stat is designed to stand alone when cropped, with type large enough to read at thumbnail size.
  - _why here:_ The sequential-reveal pattern is the biggest lever for making Meera's 'Us' page feel like a story instead of a dashboard: order days-known -> messages -> calls -> games -> rituals -> a remembered-moment highlight as a swipeable/scrollable sequence with one dominant number per screen, not a stat grid. The gamification/badges/ranking layer Wrapped also uses (leaderboards, top-percentile bragging) is the part to explicitly reject per the anti-manipulation charter — competitive/ranked framing is exactly the mechanic that turns a memory into pressure.
- **Apple Photos Memories — AppleInsider, MacRumors, Apple ML research blog on iconi** [reported]
  Memories are auto-curated highlight reels, not full logs: an on-device ML pass selects a subset of photos/events (people, places, special dates) rather than showing everything, applies a uniform visual 'look' (color/contrast treatment) across the selected set so disparate captures feel like one artifact, and pairs the result with a generated title and a matched music track whose pacing drives the edit rhythm. The user can swap the track and the whole edit re-times itself around it.
  - _why here:_ The core lesson is curation-over-completeness: a premium relationship surface shows a *selected* highlight (one remembered moment, one ritual, one funny exchange) dressed up with a consistent visual treatment, not a full chat log or exhaustive counter list. For Meera this argues for the memory graph surfacing a small, rotating set of 'best' or 'recent' remembered moments — picked, not enumerated — with a unifying visual card style (same gradient/frame family) so five different memory types read as one artifact.
- **Gentler Streak — Pixso/producthunt/healthappinsider reviews, gentlerstories.com** [reported]
  Its streak explicitly cannot break from a single missed day: a 'streak' is redefined as staying within a personalized adaptive range, and the app provides an explicit 'log a rest day' action that keeps the streak intact by design, not as an exception. Copy is written to never use loss/failure language — skipped or slow days are described supportively ('rest is essential') rather than flagged as broken.
  - _why here:_ This is the single most directly transferable pattern given Meera's charter ban on streak-loss pressure and guilt mechanics: any 'days known' or 'day count' surface must never be a breakable streak with an implied penalty — it should be a pure elapsed-time count (like an anniversary counter) with no reset condition and no 'don't lose it' framing, and any copy about gaps in contact must be neutral-to-warm, never guilt-coded.
- **Finch: Self-Care Pet — Selfpause, Autonomous.ai, screensdesign.com reviews** [reported]
  The pet (not the user) is the visible subject of care: actions are framed as 'give your bird energy by doing X' rather than command-style habit prompts, onboarding leads with hatching the pet before any feature explanation, and the pet has no death/disappear state on missed days — it just waits. Progress (growth stage, accessories) is a slow, low-resolution signal, not a daily-fluctuating number.
  - _why here:_ Reframing device: instead of 'you have talked to Meera for 47 days', a Finch-style framing makes the relationship itself the subject that grows ('what we've built together' / rel-state framing already exists in Meera's architecture) — the counters exist to feed a slowly-maturing relationship state visual, not to be stared at daily. The zero-punishment state (nothing decays, nothing dies from absence) is a second confirmation of the Gentler Streak lesson from an unrelated product category.
- **Daylio — MWM/app store listings, moodtrackers.org, calmevo reviews** [reported]
  Two concrete visual devices: (1) 'Year in Pixels' — a full year rendered as one dense grid of small colored dots, one per day, exportable as a single shareable image, which compresses a year of granular daily data into one glanceable texture rather than a chart with axes; (2) 'On This Day' — a lightweight resurfacing of a past entry from the same calendar date in a prior year, delivered inline in the current feed rather than requiring the user to navigate to an archive.
  - _why here:_ Two implementable widgets for Meera: a 'days known' visualization could use a pixel-grid (one cell per day, color intensity = contact that day) as a single glanceable artifact rather than a numeric counter alone; and a lightweight 'on this day' resurfaced-memory card (pulled from the existing memory graph / we-episodes) that appears occasionally and low-key inside the chat or the Us page, not as a push notification demanding engagement (which would risk the manipulation ban).

**Top actions:**
- Build the 'Us' surface as a single per-relationship page (not a stats tab) using Snapchat's Charms model: discrete, named, individually-tappable cards for days-known, message/call/game counts, rituals, and remembered moments — each hideable by the user without deleting the underlying memory-graph record, and none framed as a breakable streak (per Gentler Streak/Finch: 'days known' is a pure elapsed-time counter that never resets or decays, with zero loss-framing copy anywhere near it).
- Sequence the page as a short narrative reveal (Wrapped pattern) rather than a grid: order screens/sections as days-known -> a curated remembered-moment highlight (Apple Photos Memories model — algorithmically or recency-selected, not exhaustively logged, with one consistent card visual treatment) -> games/rituals -> a closing shareable card. Explicitly omit any ranking, percentile, badge, or competitive-comparison element, since Wrapped's gamification layer is the part that conflicts with the no-manipulation charter.
- Add a Daylio-style pixel-grid for the days-known history (one cell per day since day 1, shaded by contact/interaction that day) as the page's single most glanceable artifact, and a low-frequency 'on this day' resurfaced-memory card pulled from the existing we-episodes/memory graph, surfaced passively inline (chat or Us page) rather than via a notification that could read as manufactured urgency.

## How consumer products mark MOMENTS well — trigger rules, celebration anatomy, and CSP-safe open-source implementations for a Hinglish AI companion's relational memory system

- **Duolingo Engineering blog, 'You're on fire! Or, how we brought the streak milest** [verified-in-source]
  Celebration is gated to landmark day-counts only (7, 30, 100, 365, etc.) — a phoenix/flame animation that scales up and bursts into orange/yellow particles, with labels/progress/buttons staggering up from the bottom afterward. Ordinary days get zero special treatment. The team explicitly reserved the most dramatic asset (phoenix catching fire) for milestones only so 'each one registers as rare'.
  - _why here:_ Directly transferable rule for Meera's day-count/we-episode system: only fire the big celebration on named milestones (7-day, 30-day, 100-day, first call anniversary), never on every interaction — keeps it from reading as manipulative streak pressure. Also maps to a 'never stale' requirement: the flame color-shift + particle burst is a single, self-contained peak, not a persistent nag.
- **60fps.design shot library** [reported]
  Reported industry design breakdown: milestone animation redesign moved day-7 retention by roughly +1.7% in one cited case.
  - _why here:_ Directional evidence that a well-executed milestone celebration has measurable retention effect, but this is a single reported figure from a secondary blog, not independently verified — do not cite it in Meera's context/measurements.md without an n and method of your own.
- **catdad/canvas-confetti GitHub README** [verified-in-source]
  Zero-dependency, no-eval canvas particle library. Core API: confetti({particleCount, spread, origin:{x,y}, angle, colors, ticks}); ticks (default 200 frames) governs particle lifetime/fade. confetti.create(canvasEl, {resize:true}) scopes rendering to a bounded canvas element instead of full-page overlay. useWorker:true offloads rendering to a Web Worker off main thread but forfeits further canvas manipulation. Documented multi-burst patterns layer several confetti() calls with varied origin/angle/spread inside a requestAnimationFrame loop over ~2-3s to build a peak-then-decay shape.
  - _why here:_ Most viable primitive for Meera's strict-CSP self-contained web app: pure canvas draw calls, no external requests, no eval, bundleable/inlinable as a single JS file. confetti.create() on a scoped canvas is the concrete mechanism for confining celebration visuals to a milestone card in the chat UI instead of a full-screen overlay.
- **thedevelobear/react-rewards GitHub README + npm listing** [verified-in-source]
  Thin React wrapper (3.6kB gzipped, tree-shakeable, TypeScript) exposing useReward(id, type, config) where type is 'confetti' | 'emoji' | 'balloons'; the hook targets a DOM element by matching id and animates outward from it, wrapping canvas-confetti under the hood for confetti/balloons and a custom emoji-particle renderer for emoji.
  - _why here:_ Gives Meera three celebration 'flavors' out of the box anchored to a specific UI element (day-counter chip, chess win banner) rather than the whole viewport — good fit for reusing one small dependency across multiple moment-types instead of building bespoke animations for each.
- **Apple Support guide + AppleInsider + buymobiles.net** [verified-in-source]
  iMessage full-screen effects (Balloons, Confetti, Love, Lasers, Fireworks, Celebration, Echo, Spotlight) play automatically once on specific trigger phrases — 'Happy birthday'→Balloons, 'Congratulations'→Confetti, 'Happy New Year'→Fireworks, 'Pew pew'→Lasers — and fire exactly once per send, on message open, with a manual replay button available afterward (no auto-repeat, no persistence).
  - _why here:_ Concrete 'never twice automatically, replay is opt-in' trigger rule — maps directly to the charter's ban on repeated/manufactured pressure. A phrase-keyed, fire-once-then-idle model is the safe pattern for Meera's rel-state/ritual triggers: detect the moment, play once, let the user re-invoke if they want, never re-surface it unprompted.
- **Snapchat Help Center 'What do my Friend Emojis mean' + snapplanetslab.com/social** [verified-in-source]
  Snapchat separated the anxiety-inducing streak/trophy system from a private, non-competitive 'Charms' system (introduced 2019, replacing public trophies): charms are personalized mementos (birthday, snap-frequency, tenure, zodiac) rendered with the pair's own Bitmojis and visible only to the two friends, not broadcast or ranked. The streak flame + hourglass 'about to expire' warning is the loss-pressure mechanic Snapchat kept separate and has faced repeated criticism for.
  - _why here:_ Clearest cautionary/positive contrast pair for the charter's ban on guilt mechanics and fake urgency: the hourglass streak-loss warning is exactly the pattern to avoid; the private, symmetric Charms model (both-of-you-only, no ranking, no countdown) is the pattern to imitate for Meera's rel-state/we-episode milestones.
- **Chess.com Help Center 'What are the board animations on Chess.com?' + Chess.com ** [verified-in-source]
  Win celebration is a single confetti-over-board animation tied to a dedicated, independently-togglable 'Win Celebration' setting (separate from the general pre/post-game animation toggle) — fires once at game end on a win, is user-disableable, and multiple forum threads exist because users wanted to turn it off, which Chess.com honored via a discrete setting.
  - _why here:_ Reinforces that celebratory effects need an explicit per-feature opt-out, not just a global animations toggle — relevant if Meera's games centre (chess/tictactoe) adds win celebrations. Also confirms 'fires once per terminal event' as the correct trigger granularity (once per win, not per move).
- **Strava Help Center articles on 'All-Time PRs' and segment achievements** [verified-in-source]
  Strava tiers recognition: PR medals (1st/2nd/3rd personal-best time on a segment, private to the user regardless of privacy settings) vs. Trophies (2nd-10th place on the all-time public leaderboard) vs. KOM/QOM crowns (#1 public). PRs are computed and shown even without any social comparison — celebration is anchored to the user's own history, not a leaderboard, and is never revoked or re-shown as a warning.
  - _why here:_ Model for Meera's own personal-milestone detection (longest voice call, most games in a session, longest streak of calm/positive conversations) — celebrate personal-bests against the user's own history first, since Meera has no social graph and manipulation-ban rules out leaderboard-style pressure entirely.

**Top actions:**
- Implement a single shared celebration primitive using bundled/inlined canvas-confetti (or the react-rewards wrapper for the hook API) scoped via confetti.create() to the specific UI element being celebrated (day-counter chip, chess win banner, ritual-completion card) rather than a full-screen takeover — this is the only researched library confirmed dependency-free, eval-free, and CSP-compatible with no network calls, fitting the self-contained strict-CSP requirement.
- Adopt a strict 'fire-once-then-idle-with-manual-replay' trigger contract for every moment-type (milestone day-counts, chess/game wins, we-episode anniversaries): detect the qualifying event, play the celebration exactly once, never auto-re-surface it, and expose a tap-to-replay affordance — mirroring iMessage's automatic-phrase-trigger-once-plus-replay-button pattern and avoiding Snapchat's hourglass-style loss-pressure re-triggering, which is the concrete implementation of the charter's anti-manipulation ban.

## Open-source building blocks for a premium mobile-web feel (motion, confetti, sound, haptics, Rive/Lottie, bottom sheets) for a zero-animation-dep, 16MB-budget, manipulation-banned Hinglish companion app on React 19 + Capacitor Android

- **motion.dev/docs/spring** [reported]
  Motion (formerly Framer Motion) exposes a standalone spring() generator with zero React/DOM coupling: `const gen = spring({keyframes:[from,to], stiffness, damping, mass, bounce}); const {value, done} = gen.next(elapsedMs)`, driven yourself inside a requestAnimationFrame loop, writing the result to a CSS custom property or transform. Full `motion` package (React variant) is ~30kB gzip; the framework-free `animate()` mini-build is ~3.8-5kB gzip; the raw spring solver itself is a damped-harmonic-oscillator function of a few hundred lines, MIT licensed, straightforward to vendor as a single .ts file instead of an npm dependency. Native CSS spring() easing exists only in Safari/WebKit; Chrome/Android WebView (what Capacitor Android actually renders through) has not shipped it, so it cannot be used for the APK path.
  - _why here:_ The app is hand-rolled CSS with zero animation deps by design. Vendoring only the spring-solver function (not the `motion` package) gets real spring physics -- the bounce/overshoot that reads as designed-by-a-person rather than templated -- at effectively zero added bytes and zero new npm surface to audit. Because CSS spring() is Safari-only and the shipping surface is Android WebView, a JS-driven spring loop is the only option that works on the actual target anyway, so vendoring costs nothing relative to the alternative.
- **WebSearch: canvas-confetti bundlephobia/npm/github license, Aug 2026** [reported]
  canvas-confetti is a single-file, zero-dependency library, ISC licensed, ~6kB gzip (minified ~10.5kB raw). It draws to a canvas element it creates and appends to the DOM, requiring no external resources and no inline scripts beyond what you already ship -- so it does not conflict with a strict CSP as long as script-src 'self' covers your own bundle (needs no unsafe-eval, no remote fetches). It does support an opt-in `useWorker: true` mode that spins an OffscreenCanvas worker from a Blob URL, which would need worker-src blob: in the CSP; the default non-worker mode avoids that entirely.
  - _why here:_ Small enough to vendor wholesale (copy the single JS file into the repo, no build-step dependency) rather than pull from npm, matching the project's zero-deps posture. Its only CSP wrinkle is the optional worker mode; disabling it keeps the mechanism fully compatible with a locked-down CSP header. Useful for milestone/celebration UI moments (e.g. a rel-state milestone, day-count anniversary) as long as it's never wired to a false-urgency or streak-guilt trigger, which the product charter bans.
- **WebSearch: howler.js GitHub issues #505/#1294/#1014/#939, Ionic forum thread "Ho** [reported]
  Web Audio in Capacitor's Android WebView is gated by the same autoplay-unlock requirement as mobile Chrome: the AudioContext starts 'suspended' and must be resumed inside a synchronous user-gesture handler (a tap/click listener), or all playback is silently muted with no error thrown. - **WebSearch: Capacitor Haptics plugin official docs** [verified-in-source]
