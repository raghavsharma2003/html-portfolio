# A first paid expert pilot for Vyakti

Research date: 7 September 2026. Six official pages inspected, with no account creation, outreach, purchase or product trial. Public vendor descriptions establish offers and reported examples; they do not independently establish customer payment, retention, model quality or Vyakti demand.

**Recommendation, to test:** one Hindi/Hinglish/English interview-preparation or freelance-business trainer with existing paying adult clients and recurring between-session questions. Sell the expert a narrowly scoped service that reduces their answering work. Distribute a Room link through their existing cohort, email or private WhatsApp group. A shared link does not require, or imply, a WhatsApp bot integration. An audience marketplace and convincing voice imitation are not prerequisites for measuring the usefulness of approved text answers; existing product authorization and runtime gates remain prerequisites for any live Room.

## The smallest useful pilot, explicitly hypothetical

Choose one expert whose own recent records show repeated questions and whose existing clients agree to participate. Ten to twenty clients and roughly 100 eligible question threads are planning bounds, not forecast demand. Begin with the five most repeated, generic questions that fit the current published Room Q&A limit: five slots, 200 characters per question and 1,200 per answer. Do not promise broad private-document retrieval or promote private Mirror/client notes into the public catalog. Anything needing personal client context should return to the expert within the existing authorized workflow.

Use a seven-day baseline followed by a fourteen-day pilot, extending if too few comparable questions arrive. Before live access, demonstrate the real owner upload/draft/publication/reply path, source-removal behavior and each required consent/runtime gate. The private draft primitive and synthetic identity SQL proofs do not establish finished enrollment. The recently repaired two-upload finalizer serves legacy `/studio?mode=teacher` or `mode=ops`; the default modern Studio identity caller chain still needs a separate audit. Do not bypass that gap to label a live clone ready.

Measure text usefulness first through an owner-reviewed demonstration while those prerequisites are incomplete. For the eventual live pilot, log English, Hindi and Hinglish separately, including actual code-switching. The expert approves the five answers and explicitly reviews changes. Initially record the time spent checking every reply; reduce review only through an agreed, evidenced policy. Add no automatic persona updates.

| Measure | Collection and decision use |
| --- | --- |
| Avoided work | Compare comparable recurring question types with the baseline; record actual human minutes, not an assumed five minutes per AI reply. New questions created by AI availability are not automatically time saved. |
| Correction burden | Time review, edits, follow-up explanations, escalation and knowledge maintenance separately. Check that an approved correction changes the next applicable actual reply. |
| Useful resolution | Record whether the client resolved the task and whether they asked the expert again within 48 hours. Count failures and abandonments in the denominator. |
| Language and grounding | Owner labels correctness, language naturalness and unsupported claims per thread. Keep language-specific counts and examples; a pooled score can hide poor Hindi performance. A supplied source catalog is not an exact per-reply citation. |
| Adoption | Record invited, activated, returning and paying counts; ask for an explicit paid renewal after the measured pilot. Usage and compliments alone are not willingness to pay. |

Before recruiting, the expert should choose a minimum worthwhile net saving and acceptable review effort. A candidate decision rule is two hours saved per week after recurring maintenance, no unresolved privacy failure, and an actual renewal at the proposed price. These are proposed thresholds, not observed outcomes or calibrated quality standards.

## Paid adoption and unit economics to measure

A practical hypothesis is an expert-paid, capped first month, such as ₹3,000 for a defined scope and 100 question threads, delivered to their existing cohort. Present a working demonstration and service limits before requesting payment. The expert may include access in an existing membership using their current billing relationship; no new marketplace or payment integration is required to test that model. No customer has accepted this price.

**Net expert minutes saved** = comparable baseline answering minutes minus review, correction, escalation and recurring maintenance, minus an explicitly amortized setup allocation. Convert this to rupees using the expert's own valuation of freed time; freed time is not automatically booked revenue.

**Platform contribution** = collected fee minus actual model/speech charges, failed attempts/retries, allocated GPU idle time, storage/egress/database costs, payment/refund costs and support/onboarding labor. Record text and voice separately. Published competitor subscriptions do not reveal Vyakti's cost per answer.

Illustration only: a ₹3,000 fee and a 50% contribution target permit ₹1,500 total attributable cost. If support costs ₹500 and allocated infrastructure ₹300, only ₹700 remains for 100 threads: ₹7 per thread for all remaining costs. Onboarding and payment fees reduce that allowance further. These are budget assumptions, not provider prices. Measure actual tokens, audio seconds, retries, infrastructure allocation and labor before deciding whether this price works. If corrections consume the saved time, a higher usage count does not rescue the pilot.
