Not a voice clone. A person your students already know.

Voice is the part everyone demos. It is also the part that wears off in about a week if there is nothing behind it. What makes a student come back is being remembered.

It remembers each student separately. Not a chat history. A per-student record: what they keep getting wrong, what they were dreading before the last mock, what finally clicked. Rushing integration by parts is stored as a pattern with the two sessions that evidenced it, and your clone can cite them.

It stays current from your own channel. Link your channel and a new video becomes a set of proposed updates to how your clone teaches. Proposed, always. Nothing changes what your clone says to a student until you have read the change and tapped accept.

It teaches the way you teach. Strictness and warmth are separate settings, because they are separate in real teachers. Your doubt-handling ladder decides what a student gets before they get a full solution, which is also how the product refuses to be a homework machine.

It is disclosed, every time. A card before the first turn and a spoken line at the top of every call: this is an AI clone, built from your recordings, and it is not you. You see the exact wording in the studio before you publish. It cannot be shortened or switched off, including by us.

Every second of audio is signed. Watermarked twice and signed with C2PA provenance. If a clip of your cloned voice turns up somewhere it should not be, it is identifiable as synthetic and traceable to the generation that made it.

Withdrawal actually deletes. Revoking stops future use in the same transaction that invalidates the voice profile, then queues provider copies and private storage for erasure with durable retries. The studio shows you each one being confirmed, not a message saying it was requested.

Three steps, and you can see all three from inside the first.

The studio never hides what is still locked or who it is waiting on. Every step says whether it is your turn or ours, and what it costs you in minutes.

  1. Feed

    Give it something of yours

    Name the clone, confirm four permission statements you can withdraw later, and add one lecture, one voice note or one PDF of your notes. One is enough to see a draft. The file is fingerprinted in your browser before it moves, and the filename is never stored. Then you prove you are you, with a government ID and a live challenge recorded on the spot, which is what makes cloning someone else structurally impossible rather than against the rules. The raw ID and the challenge media are erased once the match is bound.

    Your turn. About fifteen minutes.

  2. Meet

    Hear it, then talk it into shape

    Review the sheet we drafted from your uploads, with every claim cited to the moment it came from, and approve it one line at a time. Hear a private preview that cannot join a call or activate anything. Pick between two candidates without being told which is which, because you cannot judge your own voice once you know the answer. Then take a calibration call with your own clone: it proposes changes while you are talking, and nothing lands on your sheet without a tap.

    Your turn, and the part teachers enjoy.

  3. Deploy

    We run the gates, then you choose where it can be reached

    Identity, intelligibility, artifacts, watermark, privacy and latency checks, plus the fidelity score against your own ceiling. If it does not clear, we refuse to activate and tell you which check failed. What clears goes to a web link, an embed on your own site, or a messaging channel. You see the student's disclosure card before you choose any of them, because that is the informed half of informed consent.

    Ours, then yours. Nothing you can do to speed the gates up, and we will not pretend otherwise.

The rules your clone cannot be talked out of.

These are not settings, and they are not instructions given to a model in a prompt. They are checks on the way out, which is the difference between a preference and a guarantee. Most of your students are sixteen or seventeen years old, and that is the reason.

  • ✓ Your clone never denies being an AI, and never implies you read or saw anything a student said.
  • ✓ Crisis helplines are guaranteed present, verified by a test that fails the build if they go missing.
  • ✓ Every student account defaults to the minor-safe tier. Engagement mechanics are structurally off, not merely disabled.
  • ✓ No streak anxiety, no manufactured urgency, no countdown to the exam. A mechanic ships only if removing every fear from it leaves it intact.
  • ✓ Your clone never speaks first without a reason it can cite. Silence is not a reason.
  • ✓ A full solution is never the first answer to a doubt. Your ladder decides what comes before it.

What we have not proven yet.

  • This is a private build. The studio is live and you can work through it today. Voice activation is still gated on checks that have not all passed, so a clone you build now may not be able to speak yet, and the studio will tell you which gate is holding it.
  • No one has run a blind listening test. 0.7753 is embedding similarity, which is a real measurement of a real thing and is not the same thing as sounding like you. The test rig exists. Until a person has sat through it, we will not tell you how it sounds.
  • We have not benchmarked ourselves against ElevenLabs or anyone else. When we do, the result goes here whichever way it comes out.
  • The first clone off a cold start takes about three minutes because the voice runtime scales to zero. You will see the real wait, not a progress bar that finishes early.
  • Deployment channels are built and not yet turned on. The web link and the embed come first, messaging platforms after.
